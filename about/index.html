About the Lab

We investigate how musculoskeletal tissues grow and respond using advanced imaging and experimental models—so kids can thrive.

Our team brings together clinical research, bone biology, and computational science to improve how pediatric musculoskeletal disease is diagnosed, monitored, and treated.

Our Approach Collaborations Funding and Affiliations

Bridging the Clinic and the Laboratory

Our research spans children participating in clinical studies and experimental models of bone growth. Clinical studies identify challenges faced by children with musculoskeletal disease, while laboratory studies uncover the biological mechanisms behind these observations. Discoveries in the laboratory inform clinical care, and clinical questions inspire new scientific investigations—accelerating the translation of research into improved pediatric care.

Why Pediatric Bone Health

Childhood and adolescence represent a unique opportunity to shape lifelong skeletal health. The majority of peak bone mass is established before early adulthood, making these years critical for preventing future fractures and osteoporosis.

By studying how bone grows, adapts, and responds to disease, injury, and treatment, our research aims to improve lifelong musculoskeletal health for children today and stronger bones for the future.

Our Research Approach

Advanced Imaging Developing quantitative imaging methods across multiple modalities.
Bone Biology Understanding how growing bone adapts to mechanical and biological signals.
Computational Science Using image analysis, biomechanics, and artificial intelligence to extract meaningful biomarkers.
Translation Transforming research discoveries into improved pediatric care.
